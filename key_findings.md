
# Key Findings Summary
## Smart City Performance Analytics — Scenario 1: Mayor's Readiness Report
**Analyst:** Aquib Azam Ansari  
**Dataset:** smart_city_dataset.csv — 5,000 cities × 15 metrics  
**City Benchmarked:** City2720 (Population: 319,006 | Tier: Medium)  
**Peer Group:** 2,951 Medium-tier cities (100K–1M population)

---

## Finding 1 — Energy Consumption is the Most Critical Gap
City2720 consumes **756 energy units** against a peer average of **547** — that is **38% above average**.
In a peer group of 2,951 cities, City2720 ranks **2,761st** for energy efficiency,
placing it in the bottom 6% of all comparable cities.
Top-performing peers (City465, City2285) average just 130–160 units.
The gap is not marginal — it represents a systemic infrastructure problem likely tied
to the absence of smart grid technology (Smart Grid Adoption score: low).

**Implication:** Energy modernisation delivers a double benefit — lower city operating costs
AND an improved Smart Infrastructure Score. It should be the first investment priority.

---

## Finding 2 — Smart Infrastructure Score is Below the 26th Percentile
City2720 scores **29.7** on Smart Infrastructure against a peer average of **42.7**.
Rank: **2,174 / 2,951** — bottom quartile of Medium-tier cities.
Top peers (City1033: 98.4, City1130: 96.8) demonstrate that cities of similar size
can achieve near-perfect scores, proving population is not a limiting factor.
The distribution chart confirms that most Medium cities cluster between 30–65,
meaning City2720 is at the left edge of the pack — not an outlier, but lagging.

**Implication:** A targeted Smart Infrastructure investment (IoT sensors, open data
platforms, smart traffic management) over 2–3 years is achievable and would move
the city from bottom quartile to near median — a competitive shift for business attraction.

---

## Finding 3 — Public Transport is a Relative Strength (but Room to Grow)
City2720's Public Transport Usage of **43.2%** nearly matches the peer average of **44.3%**
(rank: 1,509/2,951 — right at the median).
However, top-performing peers exceed **87–95%**, showing the ceiling is high.
Because transport improvement also directly reduces energy consumption (fewer private cars),
it is a leveraged investment — one spend that improves two KPIs.

**Implication:** Public transport should be Priority 3, after energy and infrastructure,
but with the understanding that it compounds the gains from Priority 1.

---

## Overall Recommendation to Mayor Sharma
City2720 is not failing — it is a mid-tier city with average liveability
(Education: 45.3, Healthcare: 43.2, Employment: 66.4%) but falling behind peers
on the modernisation metrics that attract investment and reduce costs.

**The 3-priority investment roadmap:**
1. Smart Grid & Renewable Energy — cut consumption 30%+ in 3 years
2. IoT & Smart Infrastructure — raise score from 29.7 toward peer average 42.7
3. Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) — push transport adoption above 60% in 5 years

Cities that addressed these three areas in our dataset consistently appear in the
top 20% of overall Smart Infrastructure scores within the same population tier.

---

## Methodology Notes
- Population Tier: Fixed thresholds (Small <100K, Medium 100K–1M, Large 1M–5M, Mega 5M+)
- Energy Consumption ranked ascending (lower = more efficient)
- All other metrics ranked descending (higher = better)
- Peer group = same population tier (Medium), n=2,951 cities
- Dataset: 5,000 rows × 15 columns, zero missing values
